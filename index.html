About

Engineer by training. Leader by practice.

From a first Android app to Head of Mobile

I wrote my first Android app in 2013 and moved to Flutter in 2020. Most recently I was Head of Mobile, leading a 20-member Flutter and Android team. Along the way I set the architecture for an app used by 100K+ merchants and ran delivery across several projects at once. I have also owned products end to end as a product manager, from the PRD to the store release. Outside work I answer questions on Stack Overflow, speak at developer events and help run GDG Cloud Rajkot.

Principles I Work By

Good engineering is measured by what reaches users, not by how clever the code is. I ship small, watch how it is used, and improve from there.

Ship small, learn fast

A first version in users' hands quickly, built so it never has to be thrown away.

Teams over heroes

Clear estimates, honest code reviews and steady mentoring, so delivery never depends on one person.

Architecture that outlives its authors

Modular apps that a new engineer can pick up in their first week.

AI as leverage, not a crutch

Selected Work

Products I've led, architected or rebuilt from the ground up.

E-commerce | B2B

Vepaar

The problem: Small businesses selling over WhatsApp had no simple way to run a catalogue, take orders and keep track of customers.

The hard part: Keeping the app fast for stores with large catalogues and a lot of product images.

My Role Built the app from scratch on a new multi-module architecture. The product is used by 100K+ merchants.
Outcome Frame rate went from 20-25 FPS to a steady 60, and 120 on phones that support it.
Stack: Flutter, GraphQL, Laravel
Google Play vepaar.com
Lifestyle | Social

DateDish

The problem: The product was a web app and needed a proper native app, with verified profiles, chat and post-date reviews.

The hard part: Rebuilding it in Flutter without losing features: OTP login, nearby discovery, real-time chat and subscriptions on both stores.

My Role Product manager for the rebuild. I wrote the PRD and user flows, and coordinated design, backend and QA.
Outcome Shipped on both Android and iOS.
Stack: Flutter, Node.js, Socket.io, Twilio, Stripe
Google Play datedish.com
Media | Entertainment

Live Audio Broadcasting

The problem: DJs, musicians and talk hosts wanted to go live in proper audio quality, straight to their followers.

The hard part: Connecting the app to the broadcaster's hardware over Bluetooth and WiFi, and keeping live audio stable with a custom audio library.

What I Built Added go-live notifications, real-time updates and tipping for artists.
Outcome Scaled to 50K+ concurrent listeners.
Stack: Flutter, MobX, Node.js, Socket
Pharma | Enterprise

Pharma Ecosystem

The problem: Distributors, retailers and field salesmen were juggling phone calls, paper orders and a separate ERP.

The hard part: Order booking that works without network and syncs with the ERP once the phone is back online.

Architecture Local SQLite store on the device with a background sync engine.
Outcome 30% improvement in distribution efficiency.
Stack: Android (Java/Kotlin), SQLite
Enterprise | Sales

KOOPS

The problem: Companies could not see where their field sales team was or which orders were coming in.

The hard part: Reliable GPS tracking through the working day, and order booking that works offline.

Leadership Designed and built the live tracking system.
Outcome 40% increase in sales reporting efficiency.
Stack: Android, Kotlin, Google Maps
Google Play koops.in
Networking | Smart Tech

Tapomo

The problem: Paper business cards run out, get lost and go out of date.

The hard part: Writing and reading NFC cards, and sharing a profile by tap, QR code or link on both platforms.

Architecture One profile link behind every card, so details can be updated without reprinting.
Outcome Live on Android and iOS. Users share as many cards as they like with nothing to print.
Stack: Flutter, NFC Core, Deep Linking
Google Play tapomo.com

Experiments

Small bets, shipped fast.

Small products I build quickly, mostly with AI coding tools, to test an idea in public. Some serve my community, some serve developers, and a couple exist purely for fun.

01
Experiment

Done

A to-do list based on the Eisenhower Matrix. Put each task under Do, Decide, Delegate or Delete and work on what matters first. Everything is saved in your browser, so there is no login.

Why it matters:
  • • No login, works straight away
  • • Built and shipped quickly
Stack: Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui
02
Experiment

Navkar

Made for a community drive to chant the Navkar Mahamantra 5 crore times. Play the mantra, each round adds to a shared counter, and you can pass it on with your name. In English, Hindi and Gujarati.

Why it matters:
  • • Built for my own community
  • • Spreads through sharing
Stack: Next.js, Firebase, Tailwind CSS
03
Experiment

Kshma

Make a Michhami Dukkadam card for Paryushan. Pick a template, add your photo and a message in English, Hindi or Gujarati, then download or share it.

Why it matters:
  • • Simple enough for any age
  • • Card is rendered in the browser
Stack: Next.js 15, React 19, Tailwind CSS, HTML5 Canvas
04
Experiment

DigiPin

A free, open-source API for DIGIPIN, India Post's digital address code. Send coordinates and get the 10-character code, or send a code and get the location back.

Why it matters:
  • • Open source, for other developers
  • • Serverless, no dependencies
Stack: Node.js, Vercel Functions, OpenStreetMap
05
Experiment

Win or Die

A small strategy game against a robot. You take turns removing balls from a basket, and whoever takes the last one wins. Three difficulty levels.

Why it matters:
  • • Game logic for the robot player
  • • Plain HTML and JavaScript
Stack: HTML, CSS, JavaScript
06
Experiment

EventEcho

Went to an event and not sure what to post? Pick the event, choose a tone, and it drafts a LinkedIn or X post for you. Add a photo and it writes about what is in it.

Why it matters:
  • • Gemini for text and photos
  • • Separate flows for organizers and attendees
Stack: React, Vite, Gemini API, Firebase
